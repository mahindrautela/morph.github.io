Why MORPH

Physics data do not come in one shape.

Scientific observations and simulations vary in dimensionality, grid resolution, field composition, and temporal context. MORPH treats this heterogeneity as a first-class modeling requirement rather than forcing every problem into a fixed 2D layout.

01

Modality-agnostic

A single architecture processes 1D signals, 2D fields, and 3D volumes without task-specific redesign.

02

Physics-aware fields

Component-wise convolution and field-wise cross-attention preserve interactions among scalar and vector quantities.

03

Efficient space-time context

Axial attention factorizes full spatiotemporal attention along time, depth, height, and width.

04

Transfer with less data

Pretraining improves downstream prediction under both full fine-tuning and parameter-efficient LoRA adaptation.

Results

Transfer across modalities, scales, and data regimes.

Experiments evaluate zero-shot transfer, full fine-tuning, LoRA adaptation, dataset-size scaling, and autoregressive rollouts.

01 / Cross-modality transfer

Representations learned in 2D transfer to 1D and 3D physics.

A model pretrained only on 2D incompressible Navier–Stokes shows positive zero-shot gap closure across six fluid targets, including 1D compressible flow, 3D CFD, magnetohydrodynamics, and turbulent self-gravitating flow.

GCR > 0on every evaluated target
02 / Data efficiency

Pretraining reduces the amount of task-specific data needed.

On 1D diffusion–reaction, pretrained MORPH surpasses a standalone model using 25% of trajectories. On FNS-KF, MORPH-S exceeds the full-data standalone reference with roughly 256 fine-tuning trajectories.

<1%of FNS-KF trajectories can beat the standalone reference
03 / Autoregressive prediction

Stable multi-step evolution from an initial state.

MORPH advances physical states autoregressively and produces stable ten-step forecasts on representative shallow-water and forced Navier–Stokes systems.
